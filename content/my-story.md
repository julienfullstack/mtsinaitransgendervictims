The purpose of the new website will be to give transgender patients a centralized place to document complaints involving discrimination, misgendering, failures in patient advocacy, inadequate responses to complaints, and other concerns involving transgender care at Mount Sinai.

I intend for reports to be searchable by hospital, department, and, where legally appropriate and adequately supported, individual staff member. The website will distinguish clearly between patient-submitted allegations, documentary evidence, hospital responses, and independently verifiable facts.

It will also document how Mount Sinai responds to complaints, including the dates complaints were submitted, whether they were acknowledged, how long responses took, and whether meaningful corrective action was communicated.

I intend to use search-engine optimization and potentially paid advertising so that people researching transgender care at Mount Sinai can find the project. I also have an existing audience through my software work and another product, cruush, which was created with transgender users in mind. I intend to make the project known to that audience and to transgender and LGBT communities more broadly.

This is not contingent upon Mount Sinai paying me, settling with me, or convincing me not to create the website. I am not asking for money in exchange for silence. I have decided that this public accountability project needs to exist.

What I am giving Mount Sinai is an opportunity to respond before the first version is published.

If Mount Sinai wishes to provide a statement explaining its position, I will accurately represent that response on the website. If there is a factual disagreement, Mount Sinai will have the opportunity to state its account. If there has been corrective action that I am unaware of, you may explain what was done. If you believe my account is inaccurate, you may explain specifically why.

What I am no longer willing to accept is silence.

My own case is one of the reasons I have reached this point.

I have repeatedly reported that I was misgendered immediately after gender-affirming surgery. Since then, I have encountered other transgender patients who describe strikingly similar experiences, including being misgendered by staff immediately after gender-affirming procedures.

Most recently, I spoke with another transgender woman who told me she was referred to with male pronouns after breast augmentation. Experiences like these raise an obvious question: are these isolated incidents, or are the same departments or staff members receiving repeated complaints that patients themselves cannot see?

That is precisely the kind of pattern a public reporting system could help identify.

My concerns also go far beyond misgendering.

I have been trying for an extended period to get Mount Sinai to meaningfully address what happened during my surgery and the profound consequences I have experienced since then.

My position has remained consistent: I consented to a procedure described on my consent documentation as “clitoral burial.” I did not understand myself to be consenting to what has subsequently been described as a “partial” or “incomplete” burial.

I dispute the surgeon’s account of our preoperative discussion.

My recollection is that immediately before surgery I specifically sought reassurance that the area would be completely buried and that I would wake up with the appearance I had requested. I have been saying this since shortly after I woke from surgery.

Appearance was extremely important to me. Preserving clitoral sensation was not my primary surgical goal. Had I understood that the proposed result would leave the anatomy exposed or partially exposed in the manner I ultimately experienced, I would not have consented to the procedure.

That is why the terminology matters so much to me.

I have searched for medical literature describing “partial clitoral burial” or “incomplete clitoral burial” as a defined procedure corresponding to the result I received, and I have not found literature that would have allowed me, as a patient, to reasonably understand that this was a possible meaning of the term “clitoral burial.”

If Mount Sinai possesses literature, patient-education materials, diagrams, consent materials, operative protocols, or other documentation demonstrating otherwise, I want those materials.

I also want an explanation of how Mount Sinai concludes that I gave informed consent to this specific outcome when the consent documentation I signed used different terminology.

I have contemporaneous and third-party evidence regarding my understanding of the planned procedure, including conversations with my therapist. I have also now spoken with a former patient who describes concerns involving the same surgeon that I believe warrant further examination. I expect that person may provide a statement.

These are serious factual disputes. They deserve a serious response rather than silence.

The physical consequences for me have been devastating. I continue to experience pain and unwanted sensation that interfere with ordinary activities, sexual functioning, masturbation, leaving my home, showering, and my ability to feel comfortable in my own body.

The psychological consequences have also been severe. Prolonged pain, inability to sleep, sexual dysfunction, and feeling that my reports were being ignored caused my mental state to deteriorate dramatically. There were periods in which the combination of pain and the response I received from the institution left me feeling suicidal.

I am not including that information for dramatic effect. I am telling you because it demonstrates the seriousness of what I have been reporting.

I trusted Mount Sinai with some of the most consequential medical care of my life. I moved through an enormous amount of difficulty to obtain gender-affirming surgery. I chose this surgical approach in part because I believed it would give me an appearance I could live comfortably with while avoiding other surgical tradeoffs.

Instead, I now live every day with a result I maintain that I never agreed to receive.

And when I have repeatedly tried to explain that, I have felt ignored.

Before the website launches, I am asking Mount Sinai to answer several straightforward questions:

Why have my repeated and detailed complaints not received a substantive response addressing the allegations I have raised?

What investigation, if any, has Mount Sinai conducted into my complaint regarding being misgendered immediately after gender-affirming surgery?

What investigation has been conducted into my complaints regarding my surgeon and the discrepancy between my understanding of the procedure, the terminology on my consent documentation, and the result I received?

Does Mount Sinai track repeated complaints involving transgender patients, including misgendering, discriminatory remarks, or other allegations concerning particular staff members or departments?

What corrective or disciplinary processes exist when the same staff member or department receives repeated complaints from transgender patients?

What does Mount Sinai consider “clitoral burial,” and what documentation was provided to me before surgery that would have allowed me to understand that the result I received was within the scope of the procedure to which I consented?

When will I receive the complete records and documents I have requested?

I want my records in full by Monday, September 21. If they have not been provided by the time the first version of the website launches, I will accurately document that they had not been provided as of that date.

I am also investigating legal avenues concerning my care, informed consent, civil rights, and the handling of my complaints. Those issues will be addressed through the appropriate legal processes.

Separately, the public-accountability project will continue.

I want to emphasize something because I do not want the purpose of this message misunderstood: I am not seeking a payment in exchange for abandoning this project. Money does not restore the bodily function, comfort, sexuality, or quality of life I believe I have lost.

What I want is accountability.

I want transgender patients to be able to report what happened to them.

I want repeated complaints to become visible as patterns rather than disappearing into separate private correspondence.

I want patients to know whether other people have reported similar experiences.

I want Mount Sinai to have to acknowledge complaints, investigate them, respond to them, and explain what was done.

And I want my own case addressed directly.

When this project launches, I intend to include the history of my attempts to obtain a response, including the chronology of my correspondence and whether those communications received substantive replies.

I am therefore asking one final time before launch:

What is Mount Sinai’s response to the allegations and experiences I have repeatedly reported?

Why has there been no substantive explanation addressing them?

What has Mount Sinai actually done in response?

And what, if anything, does Mount Sinai want transgender patients and the broader public to understand about its handling of my case and similar complaints?

If you provide a substantive response, I will preserve it as part of the record and accurately represent Mount Sinai’s position.

If Mount Sinai does not respond, I will accurately state that as well.

I have exhausted the avenues available to me to privately obtain acknowledgment of what happened. I am now creating a way for these issues to be documented publicly, systematically, and transparently.

I would strongly prefer that Mount Sinai finally engage with the substance of what I have been reporting.

I look forward to your response.
